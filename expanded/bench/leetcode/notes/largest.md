179 Largest Number, Medium. Input: the numbers on one line. Output: the digits, one string.
Golf `ℓʘW⟦⟦"⧺18↑⟧⍣ʘN⟧⍖"0⊇ʘN[1↑]0⊐⇤_`, 29 bytes. `ℓʘW` reads the line as words (the numbers as text). The order "a before b when a+b > b+a" is the order of the infinite repetitions a^∞, and by Fine–Wilf two numbers of at most 10 digits whose repetitions agree on 18 digits have equal repetitions, so the key is the first 18 digits of the repetition as an int (< 10^18): `⟦"⧺18↑⟧⍣` doubles the word and cuts it to 18 until it stops changing, `ʘN` parses it, and `⍖` sorts descending by it. If the first word is `0` everything is 0, so `[1↑]` keeps one word; `0⊐⇤_` prints the words in order.
Full: `full  golf 0.626s  py 0.222s  OK` (100k numbers, 40% of them cut from repeated patterns).
Gaps: no comparator sort (`⍒`/`⍖` take only an int key), so the key had to be an 18-digit number — 12 of the 29 bytes. No cheap concatenation of a list of lists (`⧺⍁` is quadratic, `‹flatten›` is 9 bytes and loses the text mark), so the words are printed one by one with a fold; and the all-zero "0" case costs 8 bytes. Slower than Python: one `⍣` loop with list allocations per number.
